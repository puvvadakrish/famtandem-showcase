# HomeBase Positioning

## What We're Building

HomeBase is a family logistics operating system for the parent who is currently carrying the household coordination load. It turns scattered school emails, sports schedules, PDFs, appointments, registrations, and last-minute changes into structured events with clear ownership.

The product should not lead as another shared family calendar. The first narrative should lead with the sharper pain: one parent is still privately tracking what is happening, what changed, what needs to be brought, and who is actually handling drop-off or pickup. HomeBase makes that hidden logistics work visible, assignable, and confirmed.

## Selected First Narrative

The first narrative should speak to **the overloaded default parent**.

This is the most resonant market entry because the pain is acute, emotional, and already named by the market. Parents describe the problem as mental load, default parenting, household admin, family logistics, and always being the person who has to remember. The buyer does not merely want a prettier calendar; they want relief from being the only person who knows what needs to happen next.

High-complexity families are still the best early-adopter segment, but they are a filter rather than the core narrative. The message should be:

> For the parent who remembers everything, FamilyOps turns family logistics into shared coverage.

Updated brand direction:

> For the parent who remembers everything, HomeBase turns family logistics into shared coverage.

## Why This Approach

Competitors cluster around shared calendars, family organizers, chores, lists, and general household command centers. Many mention mental load, but most still resolve it into "see everything in one place." HomeBase should own the next step: "know what is uncovered and who owns it."

Research signals support this direction. The 2024 Skylight Mental Load Report found that 78% of moms consider themselves the default parent and 73% feel they are the only person running the family calendar. A University of Bath and University of Melbourne study reported that mothers manage about 71% of household mental-load tasks, including scheduling, planning, and arranging activities. Competitors such as Howbout and Famnly emphasize shared calendars and visibility; newer AI products such as Nori and PAM validate the ingestion/extraction wedge, but still leave room for a stronger ownership-and-coverage position.

## Positioning Frame

Calendar apps answer:

- What is happening?
- When is it?
- Where is it?

FamilyOps answers:

- Who saw it?
- Who owns it?
- What changed?
- What is still uncovered?
- What needs a decision before the day falls apart?

## Core Promise

Never be the only one who knows what needs to happen.

## Brand Direction

Use **HomeBase** as the brand territory, but do not treat it as the recommended public name.

HomeBase better supports the broader mission than FamilyOps: helping the family function as a coordinated unit. It can start with kids' schedules and coverage, then expand naturally to grandparents, caregivers, pets, shared cars, errands, home services, travel, medical appointments, and household responsibilities. "FamilyOps" is precise but enterprise-coded; "HomeBase" feels more human, durable, and expansive.

The naming caveat is significant. Homebase is already a well-established workforce scheduling/payroll company, and there are household/family apps using HomeBase/Homebase variants. The product should preserve the HomeBase concept of "a shared base of operations for family life," but choose a more ownable public name.

## Naming Shortlist

The current best direction is to avoid names that sound like category descriptors. Family Relay was strategically accurate, but it feels more like a feature than a brand. The better path is a warm, ownable name that can carry "home as a coordinated unit," with the tagline doing the explanatory work.

1. **FamTandem**: Best practical registration recommendation after AWS checks. "Tandem" carries shared movement and shared responsibility, while "Fam" keeps it shorter than HomeTandem. Domain note from AWS Route 53 Domains: `famtandem.com` and `famtandem.app` are available. Tradeoff: "Fam" is casual, and Tandem still feels a little product-y.

2. **HomeCue**: Best brand-feel candidate, but weaker domain path. It is short, plain, and points at the real parent need: the right cue at the right time, with the right person owning it. AWS Route 53 Domains reports `homecue.com` and `homecue.app` as unavailable; `usehomecue.com` and `gethomecue.com` are available. Recommendation: use only if the team is comfortable with a prefixed `.com`.

3. **FamNudge**: Best short behavioral name. It suggests gentle prompts and reminders without sounding like another calendar. AWS Route 53 Domains reports `famnudge.com` and `famnudge.app` as available. Tradeoff: "nudge" can feel slightly annoying if the product voice is not calm.

4. **FamSignal**: Best clarity around alerts and changes. It says the product surfaces the important signal from household chaos. AWS Route 53 Domains reports `famsignal.com` and `famsignal.app` as available. Tradeoff: more functional and less warm than FamTandem.

5. **FamQueue / Family Queue**: Best inbox/backlog metaphor. It fits the family inbox, uncovered events, and "what needs attention next." AWS Route 53 Domains reports `famqueue.com`, `famqueue.app`, `familyqueue.app`, `usefamilyqueue.com`, and `getfamilyqueue.com` as available; `familyqueue.com` is unavailable. Tradeoff: "queue" can feel like support tickets, waiting lines, or task management rather than shared family ownership.

Names to keep as backups, not primary recommendations:

- **HomeTandem**: Strategically clear and AWS reports `hometandem.com` / `hometandem.app` as available, but it feels like a mouthful.
- **HomeRally**: `homerally.com` is unavailable; `homerally.app`, `usehomerally.com`, and `gethomerally.com` are available.
- **FamilyKit**: `familykit.com` and `familykit.app` are unavailable; `usefamilykit.com` and `getfamilykit.com` are available.
- **Homewardly**: Warm and emotional, but less obviously about coordination.
- **Kithline**: Ownable and meaningful once explained, but too obscure for a busy-parent product.
- **Family Relay / FamRelay**: Clear positioning language, but too literal as a brand.

Queue vs Signal vs Tandem:

- **Queue** is best for the product surface: family inbox, unresolved items, and things waiting for action. It is probably a feature name, not the master brand.
- **Signal** is best for AI/change detection: pulling the important thing out of school-email and activity-app noise. It is a strong alternate brand if the product leans hardest into detection and alerts.
- **Tandem** is best for the emotional promise: the family moving together, with shared responsibility instead of one default parent carrying the load.

Recommended direction: buy **`famtandem.com`** and **`famtandem.app`** if AWS still shows them as available at checkout. Keep **HomeCue** as the best naming alternative only if the team is comfortable with `usehomecue.com` or `gethomecue.com`.

> FamTandem turns family logistics into shared coverage.

## Alternate Promise Lines

- Stop carrying the family schedule in your head.
- Turn school emails and sports chaos into covered family plans.
- The shared logistics layer above your calendar.
- Know what is happening, who owns it, and what still needs coverage.
- From "Did you see this?" to "It's covered."
- One place for the people, plans, and coverage that keep home running.
- A shared base of operations for family life.

## Strategic Enemy

The enemy is not Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or Cozi. The enemy is the invisible coordination burden that remains after something is technically "on the calendar."

The product should avoid fighting the generic family organizer category head-on. Instead, it should make a narrower claim: HomeBase is for the moment after the schedule enters the household, when someone still has to make sure the plan is understood, assigned, updated, and covered.

## Key Decisions

- Lead with the overloaded default parent: This person feels the pain most acutely and is most likely to seek, try, and pay for relief.
- Use high-complexity families as the ICP filter: Multiple kids, activities, school emails, caregivers, and schedule changes make the problem frequent enough to validate.
- Do not position as a shared calendar replacement: Existing calendars are entrenched, and the wedge is stronger as a coordination layer above them.
- Own coverage as the differentiator: The product should consistently ask whether each event has a responsible person, a clear unresolved state, and reconfirmation after changes.
- Make source-backed AI a trust principle: Every extracted event should preserve the source and confidence level so parents can trust automation without losing control.
- Use HomeBase as brand territory, not necessarily the public name: It carries the broader family coordination mission better than FamilyOps, but the exact name is too crowded to recommend without qualification.
- Prioritize FamTandem as the practical naming direction: It has the cleanest AWS-available `.com` + `.app` pair among the stronger candidates, while still communicating shared responsibility.

## Open Questions

- Is FamTandem good enough to carry into validation, or should naming remain open while the concept is tested?
- Should the first landing-page headline use emotional relief language or operational coverage language?
- Should the first validation offer be concierge service, clickable prototype, or waitlist landing page?

## Resolved Questions

- Brand direction: Prefer the HomeBase territory over FamilyOps because it feels more human and expansive, and it can grow from child activity coverage into whole-household coordination.
- Public naming recommendation: Do not use HomeBase as the default public name because it is crowded. FamTandem is the current practical recommendation based on AWS domain availability; HomeCue remains the best brand-feel candidate if a prefixed `.com` is acceptable.
